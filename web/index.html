The network from Nvidia's 2016 paper: normalization, 5 conv layers, 3 dense layers and one output. MSE loss, Adam, dropout. Training runs in the browser with TensorFlow.js.

Record some data first, then train.
If training loss keeps going down while validation loss goes up, it's overfitting. Training loss is usually higher here because it includes side camera images and dropout.

The network steers from the center camera 15 times a second and a PI controller handles speed. If the car leaves the road it gets reset to the center line and that counts as a takeover (6 s penalty in the autonomy score).

Pressing a steering key here also counts as a takeover. If your model keeps failing at one spot, record more driving there and retrain.

Pictures are numbers

Each pixel is three numbers (red, green, blue). After cropping the sky and the hood and resizing to 66x200, the network gets 39,600 numbers. That's all it sees.

Labels

Every saved image is paired with the steering angle at that moment, same idea as Udacity's driving_log.csv. Steering goes from -1 (full left) to +1 (full right). The toy example below uses -15 / 0 / +15 to keep the numbers readable.

Gradient descent by hand

A 3x3 picture with road (8) on the left and grass (1, 2) on the right. The toy model uses one number, x = right column - left column = 4 - 24 = -20, and guesses w * x. The road bends left so the target is -15.

roundwguessmisslossslope

slope = 2 * miss * x, new w = w - lr * slope. After three rounds the guess is -14.72. The real network does the same thing with about 250k weights and thousands of images.
